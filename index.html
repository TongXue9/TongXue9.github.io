03 / Selected Work

01

Hunting a gate-driver fault during DPI

OmniVision · Direct Power Injection · 150 kHz–400 MHz

During DPI on a high-side output, the device withstood less and less injected power at higher frequencies. The output itself looked clean, but the MCU's fault line dropped.

Decoding the status registers against the datasheet pointed at the gate driver. A third scope channel confirmed it: instead of an 80% duty PWM, GD sat flat at 12 V. The series inductor in the decoupling network blocked RF from the load side, so the noise coupled into the IC.

Fix: move the 2 × 22 nF capacitors between the injection point and the IC so they shunt the RF away. The device passed the subsequent re-test.

03

Three control paths, one RoArm M3

NXP · ROS 2 Humble · MoveIt 2 · MuJoCo (Unitree SDK DDS) · ESP32 UART

Each path tests a different layer. Planning checks the high-level stack. Servo checks real-time Jacobian control. Direct DDS skips MoveIt and checks IK, joint mapping, and actuation. In simulation, all three end in the same CRC-protected LowCmd packet, streamed over DDS to a MuJoCo model of the arm. On the real arm, the same ROS driver switches to a JSON-over-UART backend for the ESP32 servo controller, and it only sends commands once valid joint feedback has arrived.
